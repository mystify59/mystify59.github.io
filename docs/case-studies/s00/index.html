01 / Designing the supports around the assembly.

During my Li Auto internship, I worked on component supports and internal structural details within the S00 team. Cameras, the microphone, display, cooling hardware and central control unit (CCU) had to share a limited envelope while remaining accessible for assembly. My work connected the overall layout to fixing positions, clearances, local reinforcement and sealing.

CCU mounting, sealing and alignment

The new CCU air duct needed both a close fit and space for adhesive foam sealing. I developed a pair of identical sealing parts mounted with the CCU, specified M4 fixing positions around the surrounding components, and shaped the internal recess around the assembly. A lower locating groove was intended to help alignment during assembly.

Microphone and head-camera supports

I separated the microphone support from the head-camera bracket, extended it and fixed it directly to the main structure. The longer span raised a stiffness concern, so I added ribs at the internal corners while retaining the central M2 fixing holes. For the head-camera bracket, I retained its position and defined a three-screw fixing arrangement; an optional alignment feature remained dependent on the manufacturing approach.

Display and cooling supports

The display sat to one side to meet the exterior form. I shifted the support opening, revised the hole positions and cut back its lower area to clear the control box, recording the remaining mass-balance question for review. The lighter fan module used an open support shaped around neighbouring hardware. These changes were documented as CAD and assembly decisions, with stiffness and balance checks kept separate.

02 / Investigating the main-frame load path in Ansys.

Alongside the local supports, I investigated how the upper chassis transferred arm and internal-equipment loads into the hip interface. I worked through the load representation, support assumptions and local mesh around the sloped shoulder transitions. The results below come from the July–August frame model and its saved Ansys runs.

Static stress and deformation

The saved 2 mm shoulder-refined model reported 5.396 MPa peak von Mises stress and 0.005878 mm maximum total deformation under the modelled gravity case. The original plots below match those saved results. The governing stress concentration was near the lower equipment-support transition, rather than the shoulder region selected for refinement.

Checking local mesh sensitivity

I selected the sloped shoulder region for local refinement, then compared 4, 2 and 1 mm settings under the same load case. This helped distinguish a mesh effect from a structural design change.

Moving from 2 to 1 mm changed the peak stress by about 2.4% and displacement by about 0.13%. The 2 mm setting was restored as the working baseline. This checks sensitivity in the selected shoulder region; the lower support hotspot still needed its own refinement.

What the model established

A separate full-mass modal solve with the rigid hip representation recorded its first three modes at 83.73, 143.31 and 215.54 Hz. These frequencies depend on the assumed joint restraint and mass representation. Changes from earlier support models were not treated as physical stiffness improvements.

The work identified where to investigate the load path and where the model needed more detail. Real joint flexibility, motion loads, fastener behaviour and the governing hotspot remained separate checks. These archived results do not establish physical validation or fatigue life.

03 / A wrist-camera mount for VLA data collection.

I also designed a wrist-mounted camera support for robotic-arm demonstrations used in vision-language-action (VLA) data collection. This was a separate fixture from the head-camera supports above. The design brief specified a 0.8 kg camera and five M8 wrist fixing locations.

My contribution covered the mount design and participation in demonstration capture. Wearable tracking and VR equipment allowed the robot arms to mirror operator movements during tasks such as organising parts. The fixture work supported that collection setup; model training and policy-performance results are outside this case study.

Reading the saved static results

These diagrams were post-processed from the saved 17 August Ansys results. For the camera-weight-plus-inertia case, the stress plot highlights the local peak near the transition into the camera housing. Displacement increases towards the camera end, while the wrist fixing regions remain constrained. Both plots retain the undeformed shape; colour shows the calculated result.

Both static cases were solved, alongside two prestressed modal and two eigenvalue-buckling studies. The camera was represented as a force without an added camera mass, so those modal studies describe the prestressed bracket model, not the complete camera assembly’s dynamics.

Limits and the next design checks

The results are preliminary because the actual camera centre-of-mass offset, joint flexibility and print-direction-dependent PLA behaviour were not validated. Fatigue life remained unresolved without an S–N curve for the printed material. Physical camera stability and fixture performance would need their own tests before accepting the mount for service.
