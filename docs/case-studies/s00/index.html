S00: internal structure & hand studies

Internal component planning, mounting and assembly details for a humanoid robot, alongside research into fixed hand postures for demonstration models.

Making the inside work together.

During my Li Auto internship, I worked on the internal layout of the S00 humanoid robot. Cameras, a microphone, the display, cooling hardware and the CCU had to share limited space while remaining mountable and accessible.

I refined component supports, fixing positions and assembly details, documenting the changes and unresolved questions in a structural review. Alongside this work, I organised a posture-reference study for fixed demonstration hands, exploring greeting, fist and relaxed forms.

Start with the surrounding assembly.

I planned supports around existing component positions and interfaces. This meant checking where hardware could attach, where neighbouring parts needed clearance, and how each local change affected the assembly as a whole.

Working with existing interfaces

In an earlier control-box mounting exploration, I worked around four existing fixing holes. I corrected the proposed pattern from a square to a cross and identified the need for a vertical extension fastening to the side of the body-frame beam.

Screen position and mass distribution

The display module sat to one side to meet the exterior form. I adjusted the support’s central opening towards the opposite side, revised hole positions and cut back its lower area to clear the control box. I flagged the remaining mass-balance question for further assessment.

Fan support and component clearance

For the lighter fan module, I used an open support frame and shaped it around the neighbouring hardware. The review documented the packaging clearance and the aim of keeping this support light.

Separate the mounts. Resolve the details.

I retained the eye-camera bracket’s position and defined a three-screw fixing arrangement. An alignment feature was considered, but left open until the manufacturing approach was clearer.

For the microphone, I separated its support from the eye-camera bracket, extended it and fixed it directly to the main structure. The longer span raised a stiffness concern, so I added ribs at the internal corners while keeping the central M2 fixing holes clear.

These revisions connected the overall layout to practical details: fastening positions, local reinforcement and access for the components being mounted.

Fit, seal and locate the CCU.

The new CCU air duct introduced a coupled problem: its geometry needed to fit the assembly while providing an interface for adhesive foam sealing. I broke this into the duct structure, its fixing points and the surrounding fit.

The documented arrangement uses two identical sealing parts mounted with the CCU. I specified M4 fixings and added corresponding positions in the main structure, avoiding the other CCU components.

Designing for assembly

I shaped the internal recess around the CCU assembly, retained space at the corners for foam, and added a lower locating groove to help alignment during assembly.

Researching how a fixed hand should read.

Alongside the internal structure, I researched hand postures for fixed demonstration hands. The brief explored moulded or 3D-printed forms for a humanoid showcase model, including open-palm greeting, closed-fist and relaxed neutral poses.

Organising the reference study

I collected references across relaxed hanging, reaching and waving gestures, then compared fist shapes and downward-motion poses. Separating whole-arm posture from hand and wrist shape helped structure the visual research.

Relaxed hand reference collected for the posture atlas · third-party source via Pinterest

The study focused on proportions from different views, finger grouping and smooth surface transitions. The brief called for continuous fixed forms, with articulated joint and mechanism details removed.

Reference image source: Pinterest.

From local parts to a coherent assembly.

Extending the microphone support raised a stiffness concern, while the offset display left a mass-balance question. Recording these alongside the CAD revisions kept the remaining checks visible as the design developed.

Further work would need to verify those points and confirm the manufacturing approach before resolving the optional alignment feature. The hand study provided a separate set of posture and proportion references for the demonstration model.
