From reference to assembly.

I developed the initial Fusion model and component architecture for a full-scale recreation of Anvil from ARC Raiders. The challenge was to translate the recognisable form into separate printable parts and an assembly that could be developed further.

ARC Raiders provides the appearance reference. The component modelling and build development form my own personal project.

Give each component a place in the build.

The original assembly contains eleven CAD components, including the outer barrel shroud, front sight, grip panels, chamber and centre shaft. The assembled and exploded views make their arrangement clear before physical fit checks.

The first full-scale iteration.

The first iteration was printed at full scale in China. That print is still in China; the images shown here document the CAD design and its development rather than the physical print.

The project remains in development until the internals are finished and tested. The first print establishes a physical iteration; fit, movement, assembly, durability and sound performance still need their own checks.

Developing the chamber.

The chamber, shaft and support arrangement is a prototype direction. Printed fit and free movement remain to be evaluated.

A later hollowing study.

A later CAD study explores retaining an outer shell, a central sleeve and a connecting web inside the decorative drum. This is separate from the sound plate and remains a CAD prototype.

Later drum and acoustic development used Codex and Fusion assistance. The current visuals support design discussion; they do not establish printed-weight savings, sound performance or physical validation.

What comes next.

Check the printed parts for fit and assembly, evaluate chamber movement, and continue sound-module prototyping. The longer-term aim is to turn a complex reference-led build into a clear making experience.
